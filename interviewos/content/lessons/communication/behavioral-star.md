## Explanation

### STAR

- **Situation** (1–2 sentences): context.
- **Task**: your goal/responsibility and why it mattered.
- **Action** (60%+): what *you* did, decisions, alternatives, how you handled obstacles.
- **Result**: outcome with real evidence; what you learned.

### Story bank

Prepare 6–8 true stories, each reusable for several themes:

| Theme | Example prompts |
|---|---|
| Technical challenge | hardest bug, complex project |
| Conflict | disagreement with a teammate/reviewer |
| Failure / mistake | something that went wrong |
| Ownership | went beyond your task |
| Ambiguity | unclear requirements |
| Learning | learned a new technology quickly |
| Feedback | received critical feedback |

### Honesty

Only claim what you did and what you know. Unmeasured outcomes: say so, describe qualitative evidence and how you'd measure. Follow-up questions quickly expose inflated metrics.

### Company context

Some companies map stories to explicit values (e.g. Amazon's Leadership Principles; Microsoft emphasises growth mindset and collaboration). Read the company's published values and choose stories accordingly.

## Worked examples

The **My Engineering Experience** section in InterviewOS seeds your three Make projects with only the facts you provided — empty fields stay empty until you fill them. Generated practice questions point out which fields are missing (e.g. responsibility, result), so your stories are built from real details rather than invented ones.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Behavioral rounds don't matter for engineers."** — They often decide between technically similar candidates.
- **"Memorise scripts."** — Memorise structure and key details; tell it naturally.
- **"Small internships have no stories."** — Debugging, learning fast, receiving feedback and scoping work are strong stories.

## Practical exercises

1. Write STAR stories for each of your three projects in **My Engineering Experience**.
2. Practise each story aloud in under two minutes, then answer three follow-up questions about it.
3. Do a behavioral mock interview in InterviewOS.
